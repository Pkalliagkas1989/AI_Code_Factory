# Marketing research bibliography

Verified against publisher search records, author copies or institutional
records on 2026-11-08. This is a targeted review, not a systematic review.
“Abstract/record” means the full paper was not independently reviewed here.
DOIs identify publications; linked open copies support inspection. Metadata
access does not imply replication, commercial applicability or endorsement.
Research applications and local tool design choices are our interpretations.
For linked papers, abstracts/excerpts and metadata were inspected; this does
not represent a full-text critical appraisal of each study.

## R01

Itani, O. S., Goad, E. A., & Jaramillo, F. (2019). Building customer relationships
while achieving sales performance results: Is listening the holy grail of sales?
*Journal of Business Research*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2019.04.048

Access: publisher abstract/search record. Basis: synthesis of listening,
adaptation and relationship/sales research; associations do not validate a
particular script. [Publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296319303017).

## R02

Packard, G., & Berger, J. (2021). How concrete language shapes customer
satisfaction. *Journal of Consumer Research, 47*(5), 787–806.
https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaa038

Access: publisher abstract/search record. Basis: customer-service field data and
experiments; relevance to the customer's need matters. [Publisher](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/47/5/787/5873524).

## R03

Neumann, N., & Böckenholt, U. (2014). A meta-analysis of loss aversion in product
choice. *Journal of Retailing, 90*(2), 182–197.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2014.02.002

Access: [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022435914000189).
Basis: product-choice meta-analysis with
variation by design and context. No universal copywriting multiplier.

## R04a

Brown, A. L., Imai, T., Vieider, F. M., & Camerer, C. F. (2024). Meta-analysis of
empirical estimates of loss aversion. *Journal of Economic Literature, 62*(2),
485–516. https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20221698

Access: [publisher abstract](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.20221698).
607 estimates from 150 articles; reported mean coefficient 1.955. This is not
an expected campaign conversion lift.

## R04b

Yechiam, E., & Zeif, D. (2025). Loss aversion is not robust: A re-meta-analysis.
*Journal of Economic Psychology, 107*, 102801.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joep.2025.102801

Access: [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167487025000133).
Reanalysis challenges robustness under unordered, symmetric gain/loss designs.
This dispute concerns decisions under risk; it does not refute every framing effect.

## R05

Lieberman, M. D., Eisenberger, N. I., Crockett, M. J., Tom, S. M., Pfeifer, J. H.,
& Way, B. M. (2007). Putting feelings into words: Affect labeling disrupts
amygdala activity in response to affective stimuli. *Psychological Science,
18*(5), 421–428. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x

Access: [author-hosted paper](https://sanlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2016/03/Lieberman_2007_PsychScience.pdf)
and publisher abstract. Laboratory emotion/neural task; no sales outcome or
validation of a salesperson inferring a buyer's feelings.

## R06

Franke, N., Schreier, M., & Kaiser, U. (2010). The “I designed it myself” effect
in mass customization. *Management Science, 56*(1), 125–140.
https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1090.1077

Access: [author/institution-hosted paper](https://www.wu.ac.at/fileadmin/wu/d/i/entrep/4_forschung/full-text-papers/franke_schreier_kaiser_2010_designed_it_myself.pdf).
Self-design can increase valuation; meaningful contribution is not a sham choice.

## R07

Fitzsimons, G. J., & Lehmann, D. R. (2004). Reactance to recommendations: When
unsolicited advice yields contrary responses. *Marketing Science, 23*(1), 82–94.
https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.1030.0033

Access: [author/institution-hosted paper](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1001/1001.pdf).
Experiments on resistance to advice; boundary conditions matter.

## R08

Jacob, C., Guéguen, N., Martin, A., & Boulbry, G. (2011). Retail salespeople's
mimicry of customers: Effects on consumer behavior. *Journal of Retailing and
Consumer Services, 18*(5), 381–388.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2010.11.006

Access: [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0969698910001165).
Retail interaction evidence; verbal and nonverbal mimicry does not isolate an
AI instruction to repeat the last words.

## R09

Priolo, D., Pelt, A., St Bauzel, R., Rubens, L., Voisin, D., & Fointiat, V.
(2019). Three decades of research on induced hypocrisy: A meta-analysis.
*Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 45*(12), 1681–1701.
https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167219841621

Access: [publisher record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0146167219841621)
and indexed abstract. 29 published and nine unpublished studies; commercial
transfer and the discomfort mechanism are not established by this synthesis.

## R10

Bernritter, S. F., van Ooijen, I., & Müller, B. C. N. (2017). Self-persuasion as
marketing technique: The role of consumers' involvement. *European Journal of
Marketing, 51*(5/6), 1075–1090. https://doi.org/10.1108/EJM-04-2015-0213

Access: [institutional abstract and citation](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/self-persuasion-as-marketing-technique-the-role-of-consumers-invo/).
Tipping field experiment and laboratory study; involvement moderated effects.
Customer-generated reasons are not proof of general software purchase effects.
The institution indexes Müller as Mueller; the DOI identifies the same paper.

## R11

Levin, I. P., Schneider, S. L., & Gaeth, G. J. (1998). All frames are not created
equal: A typology and critical analysis of framing effects. *Organizational
Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 76*(2), 149–188.
https://doi.org/10.1006/obhd.1998.2804

Access: [research-lab-hosted paper](https://worthylab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/levinetal_1998_all_frames_are_not_created_equal.pdf).
Distinguishes attribute, goal and risky-choice framing; mechanisms are not interchangeable.

## R12

Ganzach, Y., & Karsahi, N. (1995). Message framing and buying behavior: A field
experiment. *Journal of Business Research, 32*(1), 11–17.
https://doi.org/10.1016/0148-2963(93)00038-3

Access: [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0148296393000383)
and [author copy](https://www.tau.ac.il/~yoavgn/files/msg_frame_buying_behav.pdf).
Loss framing increased use among inactive credit-card customers in this setting;
not a universal negative-framing advantage.

## R13

Dillard, J. P., Hunter, J. E., & Burgoon, M. (1984). Sequential-request persuasive
strategies: Meta-analysis of foot-in-the-door and door-in-the-face.
*Human Communication Research, 10*(4), 461–488.
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.1984.tb00028.x

Access: [publisher abstract/citation](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-2958.1984.tb00028.x).
Conditional, small average compliance effects; initial agreement is not an
obligation to purchase.

## R14

Nunes, J. C., & Drèze, X. (2006). The endowed progress effect: How artificial
advancement increases effort. *Journal of Consumer Research, 32*(4), 504–512.
https://doi.org/10.1086/500480

Access: [publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/32/4/504/1787425).
Perceived progress increased persistence. This is distinct from commitment;
our requirement to show truthful progress is a conduct choice.

## R15

Fillon, A. A., Souchet, L., Pascual, A., & Girandola, F. (2023). The effectiveness
of the “But-you-are-free” technique: Meta-analysis and re-examination of the
technique. *Meta-Psychology, 7*. https://doi.org/10.15626/MP.2020.2640

Access: [open publisher article](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2640).
52 experiments, 19,528 participants; low-risk-of-bias subset (seven studies):
g = 0.11, 95% CI [−0.18, 0.40]. Positive pooled results do not settle robustness.
This is not a test of every “no-oriented” sales question.

## R16

Janakiraman, N., Syrdal, H. A., & Freling, R. (2016). The effect of return policy
leniency on consumer purchase and return decisions: A meta-analytic review.
*Journal of Retailing, 92*(2), 226–235.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2015.11.002

Access: [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022435915000822)
and [institutional record](https://latech.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-effect-of-return-policy-leniency-on-consumer-purchase-and-ret/).
Return-policy dimensions have different effects; profitability of a software
guarantee requires local costs and operational backing.

## R17

Scheibehenne, B., Greifeneder, R., & Todd, P. M. (2010). Can there ever be too many
options? A meta-analytic review of choice overload. *Journal of Consumer
Research, 37*(3), 409–425. https://doi.org/10.1086/651235

Access: [author-hosted paper](https://scheibehenne.de/ScheibehenneGreifenederTodd2010.pdf).
Near-zero average with variation; “always reduce choice” is not supported.

## R18

Chernev, A., Böckenholt, U., & Goodman, J. (2015). Choice overload: A conceptual
review and meta-analysis. *Journal of Consumer Psychology, 25*(2), 333–358.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002

Access: [publisher abstract](https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002).
Complexity, decision difficulty, preference uncertainty and goals moderate effects.

## R19

Kohavi, R., Longbotham, R., Sommerfield, D., & Henne, R. M. (2009). Controlled
experiments on the web: Survey and practical guide. *Data Mining and Knowledge
Discovery, 18*, 140–181. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1

Access: [publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1)
and [author copy](https://ai.stanford.edu/~ronnyk/2009controlledExperimentsOnTheWebSurvey.pdf).
Experimental design, power and implementation pitfalls; not proof that a
proposed campaign works. Published online 2008; journal issue 2009.

## R20

Wilson, E. B. (1927). Probable inference, the law of succession, and statistical
inference. *Journal of the American Statistical Association, 22*(158), 209–212.
https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1927.10502953

Access: bibliographic record; formula cross-checked with R21. This is a method
source, not customer data. The helper implements fixed 95% score intervals.

## R21

National Institute of Standards and Technology. (n.d.). 7.2.4.1. Confidence
intervals. *NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods*. Retrieved
November 8, 2026, from https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm

Access: official method documentation. Defines Wilson score limits for a
binomial proportion. Independent binary outcomes and the observation unit must
be appropriate; two marginal intervals are not an interval for their difference.
