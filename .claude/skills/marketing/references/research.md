# Ten principles: evidence, application and limits

Source IDs link to the [bibliography](bibliography.md), which records publication
metadata and access basis. These checks enforce reasoning quality. They do not
make all tactics mandatory or demonstrate that an AI consultant raises sales.

## 1. Tactical empathy → listening and adaptation

**Basis:** [R01](bibliography.md#r01), [R02](bibliography.md#r02): synthesis of sales
listening research plus customer-service field data/experiments on concrete
language. **Limit:** relationships and service settings do not establish that
an empathy script causes software sales.

**Apply:** customer interviews, a concern-to-response map, concrete copy using
verified customer goals and constraints. **Check:** distinguish actual quotes
from paraphrases and hypotheses; missing discovery remains unknown. **Measure:**
qualified leads, proposal acceptance and perceived understanding.

## 2. Loss aversion → test evidenced costs of inaction

**Basis:** [R03](bibliography.md#r03) product-choice synthesis;
[R04a](bibliography.md#r04a) broad meta-analysis and
[R04b](bibliography.md#r04b) conflicting reanalysis of decisions under risk.
**Limit:** context/design dependence; “losses always matter twice as much” is
not an instruction or a campaign forecast.

**Apply:** compare gain and loss variants using the same verified baseline,
timeframe, cost and risk. **Check:** cite every cost-of-delay input; include
implementation costs and the option to wait. Skip loss copy when costs are
unknown. **Measure:** incremental qualified outcomes plus complaints/cancellations.

## 3. Mirroring → reflect relevant customer vocabulary

**Basis:** [R08](bibliography.md#r08), retail interaction experiments.
**Limit:** verbal/nonverbal mimicry does not isolate “repeat the last words”
or establish effects in AI conversations.

**Apply:** use recurring customer terms in FAQs, copy and discovery questions.
**Check:** language must be sourced; reflection clarifies meaning rather than
mechanically copying it. **Measure:** useful responses, comprehension and fit.

## 4. Labeling → acknowledge, then verify concerns

**Basis:** [R05](bibliography.md#r05), laboratory affect-labeling/neural evidence.
**Limit:** indirect evidence; no purchase outcome or salesperson mind-reading.

**Apply:** “It sounds like migration effort is the concern; is that right?”
followed by relevant information. **Check:** tentative wording, observable basis,
room for correction; never infer hidden fears as fact. **Measure:** correction
frequency, objection resolution and perceived pressure.

## 5. Illusion of control → meaningful participation and autonomy

**Basis:** [R06](bibliography.md#r06) self-design/valuation experiments and
[R07](bibliography.md#r07) reactance to advice. **Limit:** product co-creation
is not equivalent to decorative choices in a sales funnel.

**Apply:** let customers choose pilot priorities, scope or success criteria.
**Check:** their choices can change the recommendation, including declining.
**Measure:** completion, decision satisfaction and pilot value.

## 6. Cognitive dissonance → customer-led examination of a goal gap

**Basis:** [R09](bibliography.md#r09) induced-hypocrisy synthesis;
[R10](bibliography.md#r10) self-persuasion in tipping/laboratory settings.
**Limit:** commercial transfer is unproven; effects depend on involvement,
and psychological discomfort is not an established sales mechanism.

**Apply:** an honest current-state diagnostic and questions that invite reasons
both to change and to stay. **Check:** any inconsistency refers to an actual
customer statement; alternatives include solving the problem without buying.
**Measure:** decision clarity, qualification and eventual product fit.

## 7. Framing → equivalent facts, relevant presentation

**Basis:** [R11](bibliography.md#r11) distinguishes framing types;
[R12](bibliography.md#r12) inactive credit-card customer field experiment.
**Limit:** context-specific loss-frame advantage; no universal winning frame.

**Apply:** compare time, reliability or cost presentations of the same offer.
**Check:** keep total price, denominators, conditions and timeframes equivalent;
show material costs and risks in every variant. **Measure:** comprehension and
qualified purchases with refunds/regret as guardrails.

## 8. Commitment/consistency → voluntary steps with independent value

**Basis:** [R13](bibliography.md#r13) conditional sequential-request effects;
[R14](bibliography.md#r14) persistence through endowed progress.
**Limit:** progress is a different mechanism from consistency; neither means
that a small agreement guarantees a purchase.

**Apply:** useful assessment → demo → pilot, with truthful progress feedback.
**Check:** disclose each step's effort/cost and make stopping easy. Earlier
agreement never creates a duty to buy. **Measure:** activation, pilot success,
retention and cancellations rather than empty signups.

## 9. Power of no → respect refusal

**Basis:** [R15](bibliography.md#r15), preregistered compliance meta-analysis with
positive overall and inconclusive low-bias-subset findings.
**Limit:** uncertain robustness; no validation of every no-oriented question.

**Apply:** an easy decline, permission-based follow-up and genuine not-suitable
outcomes. **Check:** refusal is respected in behavior as well as wording.
This is a conduct rule regardless of conversion impact. **Measure:** qualified
conversions, opt-outs, complaints and perceived pressure.

## 10. Certainty → reduce avoidable buying uncertainty

**Basis:** [R16](bibliography.md#r16) retail return-policy synthesis;
[R17](bibliography.md#r17) and [R18](bibliography.md#r18) choice-overload syntheses.
**Limit:** the prospect-theory certainty effect is narrower than “clarity sells”;
returns evidence does not prove SaaS guarantees pay off, and fewer choices are
not always better.

**Apply:** transparent scope, total costs, exclusions, responsibilities, relevant
proof and feasible pilot/return terms. **Check:** remaining uncertainty is visible;
guarantees require operational and economic support. **Measure:** comprehension,
abandonment, purchases, support demand, refunds and net contribution.

## Assignment evidence record

For every material recommendation record: customer observation/source, publication
ID and finding, study setting, transfer limitation, proposed application,
credible alternative, comparison/test, primary metric, customer guardrails and
decision rule. Research is supporting rationale; measured local outcomes decide
whether to continue. Use [measurement](measurement.md) for tests and analysis.
